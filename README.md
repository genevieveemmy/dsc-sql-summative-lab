# Retail Customer & Inventory Analysis (SQL & Python)

## Executive Summary
This project analyzes a wholesale miniature model business database using **SQLite3** and **Pandas**. The goal is to solve key business problems regarding customer segmentation, credit risk, target marketing, inventory allocation, and product line strategy.

---

## Technical Stack & Libraries
* **Language:** Python 3
* **Database Engine:** SQLite (`sqlite3`)
* **Data Manipulation:** `pandas`
* **Data Visualization:** `matplotlib.pyplot`, `seaborn`

---

## Notebook Structure & Key Tasks

### Step 1: Database Connection & Setup
* Connected to the `data.sqlite` database using SQLite and Pandas.
* Established an environment to execute SQL queries directly into Pandas DataFrames for quick manipulation and plotting.

### Step 2: High-Credit California Target Campaign
* **Objective:** Identify existing California-based store accounts with high purchasing power to target for a high-value, limited-edition product (San Francisco Cable Car model).
* **Action Taken:** Queried the `customers` table for stores located in California (`state = 'CA'`) with a converted numeric credit limit exceeding $25,000.
* **Result:** Filtered down to 10 high-value target accounts in California (led by top distributors like Mini Gifts Distributors Ltd.).

### Step 3: International "Collectable" Marketing Campaign
* **Objective:** Identify non-US partners for a "collect them all" thematic marketing campaign.
* **Action Taken:** Query filtered for international customers (`country != 'USA'`) whose company names contained the string pattern `"collect"` (case-insensitive wildcards `%collect%`).
* **Reflection:** Used the `!=` operator to explicitly exclude domestic (US) clients and the `LIKE` operator with leading and trailing `%` wildcards to capture variations of the word "Collect" (e.g., *Collectors*, *Collections*, *Collectables*) regardless of position in the company name.

### Step 4: USA State-Level Credit Limit Analysis (Visualized)
* **Objective:** Evaluate regional purchasing power to optimize inventory distribution and credit policies across US states.
* **Action Taken:** Grouped US customers by `state`, calculated the average credit limit per state, and sorted results in descending order.
* **Visualization:** Created a horizontal bar plot (`state` vs. `average_creditLimit`).
* **Key Finding:** New Hampshire (NH) recorded the highest average credit limit (~$114,200), followed by New York (NY) and Pennsylvania (PA), providing clear priority regions for inventory distribution.

### Step 5: Top 10 Accounts by Total Revenue (Visualized)
* **Objective:** Identify top customer accounts to prepare personalized communications for the company's upcoming 10-year anniversary.
* **Action Taken:** Executed an inner `JOIN` between `customers` and `payments` on `customerNumber`, aggregated total spent using `SUM(amount)`, and selected the top 10 accounts.
* **Visualization:** Rendered a Seaborn bar chart illustrating revenue generated by top accounts.
* **Key Finding:** **Euro+ Shopping Channel**  ($715.7K) and **Mini Gifts Distributors Ltd.**  ($584.2K) are the company's two largest revenue drivers by a significant margin.

### Step 6: High-Volume Customer Purchases
* **Objective:** Recommend catalog additions and cross-sell opportunities by identifying products repeatedly bought in bulk.
* **Action Taken:** Joined `customers`, `orders`, `orderDetails`, and `products`. Grouped by customer and product name, filtering with a `HAVING` clause for total ordered units ≥ 10, sorted in ascending order.
* **Result:** Identified 2,531 customer-product combinations reflecting sustained bulk demand.

### Step 7: Product Line Demand & Portfolio Optimization (Visualized)
* **Objective:** Evaluate product line diversity versus actual order demand to guide product development, marketing focus, and inventory stocking levels.
* **Action Taken:** Joined `products` and `orderDetails`, grouping by `productLine`. Counted distinct product offerings (`COUNT(DISTINCT productName)`) alongside total quantity ordered (`SUM(quantityOrdered)`).
* **Visualization:** Plotted total quantity ordered against the number of unique offerings per product line.
* **Key Findings:**
  1. **Classic Cars** is the highest-performing line (37 unique products, 35,582 units ordered), followed by **Vintage Cars** (24 products, 22,933 units).
  2. A direct correlation exists between catalog diversity (number of products offered) and overall volume sold across lines.

## Key Insights & Business Recommendations
1. **Targeted Promotions:** Launch the new limited-edition product exclusively to top-tier California accounts (e.g., Mini Gifts Distributors Ltd., Collectable Mini Designs Co.).
2. **Inventory Prioritization:** Prioritize US warehouse stock allocation toward New Hampshire, New York, and Pennsylvania due to higher state credit averages.
3. **VIP Retention:** Focus 10th-anniversary appreciation rewards on Euro+ Shopping Channel and Mini Gifts Distributors Ltd.
4. **Catalog Expansion:** Continue expanding offerings within **Classic Cars** and **Vintage Cars**, as deeper product diversity directly yields higher overall order volumes.
